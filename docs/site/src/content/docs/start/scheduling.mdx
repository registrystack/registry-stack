If people must book a time at a counter, a station, or a field visit, and two of them must never
get the same place, [Registry Scheduling](../../reference/glossary/#registry-scheduling) publishes
what can be booked and keeps every booking in one ledger.

## What a caller gets

A caller reads the [offerings](../../reference/glossary/#offering) a deployment publishes and the
free starts for one of them. It takes a short [hold](../../reference/glossary/#hold) while a person
confirms, then turns the hold into an [appointment](../../reference/glossary/#appointment), which
it can later reschedule or cancel. A retry under the same idempotency key returns the first
answer, never a second booking.

## What you define

You write a booking policy in YAML: the services offered, how long each appointment lasts and how
far ahead it can be booked, the opening hours, public holidays, how long a hold lasts, and when
reminders are due. Beside it, a records file lists the supply the policy books from: locations,
[resource pools](../../reference/glossary/#resource-pool) of interchangeable stations or staff, and
arrival windows. `schedulingctl` checks both offline, replays your
[fixtures](../../reference/glossary/#scheduling-fixture) against them, and packages the policy under
a digest.

## What runs

`scheduling` is an HTTP service that keeps the ledger in PostgreSQL 17 or newer and verifies tokens
from your own OpenID Connect issuer. Every hold and appointment is claimed inside one transaction,
so two callers racing for the last station get one appointment and one refusal. Reminder intents
and appointment events are delivered afterwards, from an outbox.

## What Scheduling refuses

Scheduling refuses a booking without a [task grant](../../reference/glossary/#task-grant) for that
service, location, and action, a start outside the published hours, a booking made against a policy
revision that has changed, and a start with no free capacity.
[How Registry Scheduling works](../../explanation/how-scheduling-works/) explains each boundary.

## When it is not the right product

Scheduling does not decide who may have a service; the system that owns that rule does, and
issues the grant. It is not a work queue, which is [Registry Casework](../casework/), and it sends
no messages: a reminder leaves as data for a system such as
[Registry Messaging](../messaging/) to deliver.

## Where to start

[Prove your first booking policy](../../tutorials/first-scheduling/) creates a project, checks it,
and runs its fixtures offline in about 10 minutes, with no database.
