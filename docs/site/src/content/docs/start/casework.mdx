If people in your institution must answer or approve something before another system acts on it,
[Registry Casework](../../reference/glossary/#registry-casework) gives them that review and returns
their answer to the system that asked.

## What a producer gets

A [producer](../../reference/glossary/#producer) is the system that needs the answer. It sends a
[ReviewRequest](../../reference/glossary/#review-request) naming the subject and the policy to
review it under, and later reads one [ReviewResult](../../reference/glossary/#review-result): the
outcome, any public reason, and the exact subject and policy it answers. The result never names
the reviewer. A retry under the same idempotency key returns the first request, not a second.

## What you define

You write a [ReviewPolicy](../../reference/glossary/#review-policy) in YAML: the stages a request
passes through, which people decide each stage and how many must approve, the outcomes a
[Decision](../../reference/glossary/#decision) may record, what reviewers see, which producers may
ask, and the deadlines that apply. `caseworkctl` checks the policy offline and pins it to a digest
before any request uses it.

## What runs

`casework` is an HTTP service that keeps review state in PostgreSQL and verifies tokens from your
own OpenID Connect issuer. Reviewers claim and decide tasks through its API, so your application
supplies their screens. A request stays `reviewing` until a decision settles it as approved,
rejected, changes-requested or answered, or until its producer cancels it or supersedes it with a
replacement for the same subject and policy.

## Who is allowed to act

Three parties authenticate independently. The producer is named under `trustedProducers`, bound to
an issuer, a subject, the source namespaces it may name, and the policies it may ask for. The human
reviewer is a person holding a staff or supervisor access profile. The downstream executor is
whatever system later acts on the answer, under its own authority.

Each party sends its own token and selects one
[access profile](../../reference/glossary/#access-profile) per request. Casework never combines two
profiles, and a producer's authority never supplies a reviewer's. A producer may read its result
but never the human work, and only a staff or supervisor profile can decide.

## What a request binds

Casework freezes the policy and subject a request names when it accepts it, so a later policy
change never reinterprets admitted work. A pinned version and digest that are not the active ones
are refused with `review.policy-mismatch`. The
[API reference](../../reference/apis/registry-casework/#policy-pins-and-retries) lists the subject
fields and the `submissionDigest` the acceptance returns.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-review-protocol/src/lib.rs, PolicySelection::matches,

## Where the reviewer's context comes from

With `context.mode: submitted`, the producer sends a snapshot that Casework validates against the
policy's `displaySchema` and keeps. With `context.mode: current-source`, the policy names a
[context provider](../../reference/glossary/#context-provider), the source's read-only review
interface. Casework keeps an exact reference and task metadata, never the source's data, and asks
the source again under the reviewer's own credential each time a task opens. When the source
conceals the subject or its version moved, Casework refuses the read rather than show stale data.
Either way the source keeps its data and its disclosure.

## Who decides, and when a request settles

A policy's [stages](../../reference/glossary/#stage) run in order. Each names its
[queue](../../reference/glossary/#queue), the profiles that may decide, and how many independent
reviewers must approve. When a stage becomes active, Casework creates a
[ReviewTask](../../reference/glossary/#review-task). A person reaches it only as a member of a
[team](../../reference/glossary/#team) that serves the queue in the
[directory](../../reference/glossary/#directory), holding a deciding profile, and not excluded as
the initiator or an earlier stage's reviewer. The policy declares which queues and teams exist.
The directory, which an administrator keeps at runtime, says who is in each team.

Approvals that meet a stage's threshold advance the request, or settle it as approved at the last
stage. A rejection, a request for changes, or an answer settles it at once. A decision made on a
stale read of a task is refused, and a [clock](../../reference/glossary/#clock) never decides an
outcome. [Author a Casework policy](../../configure/casework/) covers claims, fencing and clocks.

## What comes back, and for how long

The ReviewResult repeats the subject, the policy binding, and the `submissionDigest`, so the
producer can match it to its request. It adds a terminal `status`, the `outcome`, any public reason,
and any structured result the policy requires. It never carries the reviewer's private reason,
which stays in Casework's
[accountability record](../../reference/glossary/#accountability-record).

The policy sets two retention windows: how long the producer can read the result, and how long the
record of who decided is kept. [Casework retention](../../operate/casework-retention/) describes
both.

## What Casework refuses

Casework refuses an untrusted producer, a stale policy pin, an ineligible reviewer, and a
decision against a changed task.

## When it is not the right product

Casework is for human judgement. A check a program can make from data belongs in the producer or
in a [Registry Evidence](../../reference/glossary/#evidence-gateway) assertion, and a change to a
registry stays with that registry, which decides whether to apply an approved review. Casework
holds no write or apply authority over the source, so a review never becomes a change there by
itself.
