[Registry Messaging](../../reference/glossary/#registry-messaging) sends one message to one destination over one channel for an authorized
caller. It renders the message from a reviewed template, hands it to a provider the operator
configured, and reports what is known about delivery.

Why and when to send, to whom, and whether the person agreed to be contacted stay with the
caller's source of record. Messaging decides none of them.

{/* Evidence: products/messaging/README.md;
    crates/registry-messaging-core/src/access.rs, AccessProfile. */}

## What you work with

A package is the reviewed configuration a deployment serves. Three of its parts carry most of the
work:

- A template renders the message from the data a request carries, in each locale it declares.
- A sender profile fixes a channel, email or SMS, to one provider and one sender identity.
- An access profile names the callers allowed to send, the sender profiles and templates they may
  use, and how fast.

A caller picks a sender profile and a template, and sends the recipient and the data. Messaging
answers `202` once the message is queued, then tracks two facts: what it did with the message, and
what the provider reported afterwards.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-messaging-core/src/package.rs, MessagingProject, SenderProfile,
    and Channel; crates/registry-messaging-core/src/wire.rs, MessageDispatch and MessageReport. */}

## What it is not

Messaging is not a contact directory, a consent registry, or a scheduler. It holds no recipient
beyond what a request carries, and it keeps rendered content and contact details only as long as
the operator's retention allows.

Being allowed to act on a record elsewhere does not let a caller send. Every product reaches
Messaging over its public HTTP contract, and Messaging's own access profile decides. It never
writes to another product's database.

    products/messaging/scripts/check_dependency_direction.py. */}

## Try it locally

`messagingctl dev` runs the starter package on your machine, with Mailpit to catch email and a
mock SMS gateway that reports delivery.
