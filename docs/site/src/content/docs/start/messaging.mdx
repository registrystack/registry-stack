[Registry Messaging](../../reference/glossary/#registry-messaging) sends one message to one destination over one channel for an authorised
caller. It renders the message from a reviewed template, hands it to a provider the operator
configured, and reports what is known about delivery.

{/* Evidence: products/messaging/README.md;
    crates/registry-messaging-core/src/access.rs, AccessProfile. */}

## What you work with

You define a package, and an operator defines a runtime file. A package is the reviewed
configuration a deployment serves unchanged. Three of its parts carry most of the work:

- A template renders the message from the data a request carries, in each locale it declares. It
  has a JSON Schema for that data, and it can carry a sample that `messagingctl check` renders.
- A sender profile fixes a channel, email or SMS, to one provider and one sender identity. It says
  how a failed send is retried, how long a message may wait, and what happens when a send may have
  reached the provider.
- An access profile names the OAuth clients it admits, the scopes their tokens carry, the sender
  profiles and templates a sender may use, and the rate limits that apply.

The package declares each provider's type, `smtp` or `http`, and whether the provider deduplicates
a repeated send. For an `http` provider it also holds the request scripts and says whether the
provider reports delivery. The endpoint, credentials, and callback secret belong to the runtime
file, together with the database, the token issuer, the audit journal, and retention.

A caller picks a sender profile and a template, and sends the recipient and the data. Messaging
answers `202` once the message is queued. A caller that lost that answer sends the same body under
the same `Idempotency-Key`. It gets the original answer back while Messaging keeps the receipt, 7
days by default, and never a second message. An access profile can also let a caller send its own
content with no template (`allowDirectContent`).

## What a message is once accepted

Before it answers `202`, Messaging resolves the caller's access profile, checks the data against
the template's schema, renders every part, and stores the message with its recipient and rendered
content. Nothing renders later, so a package change never alters a message already accepted.

A worker then sends it. The message carries two separate facts. Dispatch is what Messaging did, and
`submitted` means the provider accepted the message. Report is what the provider said afterwards.
`status` reads `delivered` only when the provider reported it.

Whether a message can reach `delivered` depends on the provider, not the channel. An `http`
provider whose `provider.yaml` sets `capabilities.receipts: callback` posts reports that move the
status on, for SMS or email. Any other provider reports nothing, so an email sent through SMTP stays `submitted`. A
send that failed in a way a later attempt may overcome is retried with growing delays. It stops
when it succeeds, runs out of attempts, or expires. A permanent refusal is not retried. The
[message status](../../reference/apis/registry-messaging/#message-status) and the
[dispatch policy](../../reference/messaging-configuration/#dispatch-policy) hold the states and the
defaults.

## Who owns each answer

Three parties own three different answers:

- The caller's source of record owns why and when to send, the recipient, and whether the person
  agreed to be contacted. Messaging decides none of them.
- Messaging owns rendering, the queue, attempts, receipts, retention, and its audit journal.
- The provider owns delivery. Messaging repeats what the provider reported and never more.

One state belongs to nobody yet. `unknown` means a send may have reached the provider and nobody can
tell. Under the sender profile's default `onUncertain: hold`, the worker sends nothing more until an
operator who has asked the provider settles it.

## What it is not

Messaging is not a contact directory, a consent registry, or a scheduler. It holds no recipient
beyond what a request carries. It erases rendered content and contact details when the retention
period ends, counted from when the message finishes. A message still waiting or held as `unknown`
keeps them.

Being allowed to act on a record elsewhere does not let a caller send. Every product reaches
Messaging over its public HTTP contract, and Messaging's own access profile decides. An
authorisation the caller holds in another product does not carry over. Messaging never writes to
another product's database.
