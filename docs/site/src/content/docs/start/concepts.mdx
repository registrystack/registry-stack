If you have not chosen a product yet, this page shows what each one does and how they combine.

## What every registry needs

Every registry holds data behind an API with permissions and accounts for every change. It shares
answers with other services rather than copies of its records. It supports the people who review
requests and the people who must be informed.

Registry Stack offers a base for a registry that does not exist yet, and components an existing
registry adds without being replaced. The usual first component shares limited data securely: an
[Evidence Gateway](../../reference/glossary/#evidence-gateway) answers one question from your
records with a signed [assertion](../../reference/glossary/#assertion) instead of the record.

## The stack in four questions

- **What you define.** Each product reads a project you keep in version control: YAML files and,
  for some products, small Rhai scripts. Tooling checks, tests, and packages it offline.
- **What exists when it runs.** Each product runs as its own service with its own deployment
  contract, and each one is optional.
- **What a product owns.** Its own state, its audit record, and who may call its API.
- **What no product owns.** Legal authority, data ownership, eligibility, and institutional
  approval, which stay with the responsible institutions.

## What each product does

| Product | What you define | What exists when it runs | What it owns |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Base Registry Engine (BReg)](../breg-quickstart/) | A [registry project](../../reference/glossary/#registry-project): [Record types](../../reference/glossary/#record-type), [Actions](../../reference/glossary/#action), [access profiles](../../reference/glossary/#access-profile) | A REST API over a PostgreSQL database | Records, revision history, [Proposals](../../reference/glossary/#proposal), the [audit journal](../../reference/glossary/#audit-journal) |
| [Evidence Gateway](../evidence-quickstart/) | An Evidence project: assertion definitions, sources, fixtures | A service that answers each [requirement](../../reference/glossary/#requirement) from a packaged bundle | Signing, minimum disclosure, its audit trail |
| [Registry Casework](../casework/) | A [ReviewPolicy](../../reference/glossary/#review-policy) | [ReviewTasks](../../reference/glossary/#review-task) for authorized people | Decisions and the [ReviewResult](../../reference/glossary/#review-result) |
| [Registry Scheduling](../../reference/apis/registry-scheduling/) | Offerings over supply the operator anchored | Holds and appointments, each made under a task grant | Its capacity ledger |
| [Registry Render](../registry-render/) | A template package | A rendered document, byte-stable for the same inputs | Rendering and its audit entries |
| [Registry Discovery](../../explanation/discovery-as-an-index/) | An operator-approved origin list | One immutable index of provider descriptions | The index, never trust in a provider |
| [Registry Manifest](../../products/registry-manifest/) | A manifest of what a registry exposes | Static artifacts from its renderers | Validation and rendering of metadata |

## How products compose

Products compose over their public APIs, and none inherits another's permissions. Evidence Gateway
may read a BReg route as a source with its own credentials and gains none of that route's
authorization. Casework may show reviewers a BReg Proposal, and BReg still decides whether to
apply it. No product writes to another product's database.

## Where the stack sits

Where a country already runs an exchange layer or integration bus, such as X-Road or a
GovStack-style exchange, Registry Stack sits behind it. Registry Stack adds the registry-facing
surface behind that layer; it does not implement the exchange layer itself or claim conformance to
it.
