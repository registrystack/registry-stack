Every Registry Stack product follows the same model. Learn it once and each product's pages read
as a variation on it.

## What you define

A product reads a project you keep in version control: YAML files and, for some products, small
Rhai scripts. For Base Registry Engine that project declares
[Record types](../../reference/glossary/#record-type), [Actions](../../reference/glossary/#action),
and [access profiles](../../reference/glossary/#access-profile). For Evidence Gateway it declares
the questions the gateway may answer and the sources it reads. For Registry Casework it is a
[ReviewPolicy](../../reference/glossary/#review-policy).

No product has a built-in business model. A vehicle, a licence, or a residence region is
something your project declares, never something the runtime knows about.

## What runs

Each product runs as its own service with its own deployment contract, and every one of them is
optional. Before a service opens its listener, its tooling checks, tests, and packages the
project offline, and the service refuses a package that fails those checks. An institution with a
registry already in production adds one product beside it; an institution without one starts from
a Base Registry Engine project.

## What a product owns

A product owns its own state, its [audit journal](../../reference/glossary/#audit-journal), and
the decision about who may call its API. Base Registry Engine owns records, revision history, and
[Proposals](../../reference/glossary/#proposal). Evidence Gateway owns the
[assertion](../../reference/glossary/#assertion) it signs and the minimum disclosure it holds to.
Registry Casework owns review decisions and the
[ReviewResult](../../reference/glossary/#review-result). Registry Scheduling owns its capacity
ledger. Each product decides every call under its own access rules and records what it decided.

## What no product owns

Legal authority, data ownership, eligibility, and institutional approval stay with the responsible
institutions. A signed assertion states what a source held at one moment; whether that is enough
for a decision is the relying party's call. A review result records what reviewers decided; what
happens next stays with the producer that asked for the review.

## How products compose

Products compose over their public APIs, and none inherits another's permissions. Evidence
Gateway may read a Base Registry Engine route as a source with its own credentials and gains none
of that route's authorization. Casework may show reviewers a Base Registry Engine Proposal, and
the registry still decides whether to apply it. Scheduling may publish bookable supply for work
another product governs, and that product gains no say over Scheduling's ledger. No product writes
to another product's database.

## Where the stack sits

Where a country already runs an exchange layer or integration bus, such as X-Road or a
GovStack-style exchange, Registry Stack sits behind it. It adds the registry-facing surface behind
that layer; it does not implement the exchange layer or claim conformance to it.
