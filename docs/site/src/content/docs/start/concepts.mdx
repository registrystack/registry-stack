Every Registry Stack product follows the same model. Learn it once and each product's pages read
as a variation on it.

## What you define

A product reads a project you keep in version control: YAML files and, for some products, small
Rhai scripts. For Base Registry Engine that project declares
[Record types](../../reference/glossary/#record-type), [Actions](../../reference/glossary/#action),
and [access profiles](../../reference/glossary/#access-profile). For Registry Evidence it declares
the questions the gateway may answer and the sources it reads. For Registry Casework it is a
[ReviewPolicy](../../reference/glossary/#review-policy).

No product has a built-in business model. A vehicle, a licence, or a residence region is
something your project declares, never something the runtime knows about.

## What runs

Each product has its own deployment contract, and every one of them is optional. Most run as their
own service. Registry Manifest is a command-line tool. Before a service opens its listener, its tooling checks, tests, and packages the
project offline, and the service refuses a package that fails those checks. An institution with a
registry already in production adds one product beside it; an institution without one starts from
a Base Registry Engine project.

## What a product owns

A product owns its own state, its [audit journal](../../reference/glossary/#audit-journal), and
the decision about who may call its API. Registry Discovery is the exception: it serves a public,
read-only index and keeps no journal. Base Registry Engine owns records, revision history, and
[Proposals](../../reference/glossary/#proposal). Registry Evidence owns the
[assertion](../../reference/glossary/#assertion) it signs and the
[minimum disclosure](../../reference/glossary/#minimum-disclosure) it holds to.
Registry Casework owns review decisions and the
[ReviewResult](../../reference/glossary/#review-result). Registry Scheduling owns its capacity
ledger. Each product with protected calls decides every one under its own access rules and
records its authorisation decisions.

## What no product owns

Legal authority, data ownership, eligibility, and institutional approval stay with the responsible
institutions. A signed assertion states what a source held at one moment; whether that is enough
for a decision is the relying party's call. A review result records what reviewers decided; what
happens next stays with the producer that asked for the review.

No maintained product is a payment product, and none has an operation specific to payments.
Payment policy and provider contracts stay with the institution. A Registry Coordinator workflow
step can send one configured POST command to a provider through the generic
[`external.command/v1`](../../configure/coordinator/) operation. When the provider answers 429 or 503,
Coordinator can repeat the command with the same `Idempotency-Key`, within the step's attempt limit
and the run's deadline.

## How products compose

Products compose over their public APIs, and none inherits another's permissions. Registry Evidence
may read a Base Registry Engine route as a source with its own credentials and gains none
of that route's authorisation. Casework may show reviewers a Base Registry Engine Proposal, and
the registry still decides whether to apply it. Scheduling may publish bookable supply for work
another product governs, and that product gains no say over Scheduling's ledger. No product writes
to another product's database.

## Which product serves which exchange

The products serve these exchanges with another institution, another system, or a person.
The table does not list every exchange that a product serves.

| Exchange | Product | What the product does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Another institution needs one fact about your records | [Registry Evidence](../../explanation/how-evidence-works/) | Signs an answer to one predefined question, with only the outputs its assertion definition declares and not the record |
| A partner reads or changes your records | [Base Registry Engine](../../explanation/configuration-defined-registry/) | Serves a record API and decides every call under your access profiles |
| You need a fact from an institution that runs Registry Evidence | [Registry Evidence, with your application as the caller](../../tutorials/request-evidence-from-an-application/) | Returns a signed answer that your application verifies and keeps |
| You load data from another system | [Registry Adapter ingestion job](../../configure/adapter-jobs/) | Reads bounded source pages and delivers a reviewed projection to a destination |
| A standard or a national system fixes the API you must serve | [Registry Adapter facade](../../configure/adapter/) | Serves a declared API by translating each request to the source system |
| A person carries the proof themselves | [Evidence with OID4VCI delivery](../../configure/evidence-oid4vci/) | Delivers a holder-bound credential to the person's wallet |

The job host that runs an Adapter ingestion job, `adapter-job`, is not in a numbered release.
You [build it from source](../../operate/adapter/#build-the-job-host).

## Where the stack sits

Where a country already runs an exchange layer or integration bus, such as X-Road or a
GovStack-style exchange, Registry Stack sits behind it. It adds the registry-facing surface behind
that layer; it does not implement the exchange layer or claim conformance to it.
