If you are connecting Registry Casework to the systems around it, or deciding whether it fits,
these questions settle the model, asked of the project from your first review.

## Who calls Casework?

`caseworkctl explain` reports what the project compiles to, including who may call:

```sh
caseworkctl explain tutorial-work/casework
```

```json test-excerpt
{
  "authority": "producer, human reviewer and downstream executor authenticate independently",
  "contextProviders": []
}
```

The producer is the system that sends a ReviewRequest; in the project it is the `requester` under
`trustedProducers`. The human reviewer is a person holding a staff or supervisor access profile.
The downstream executor is whatever system later acts on the answer, under its own authority.
Each sends its own token and selects one access profile per request; Casework never unions two
profiles. An administrator maintains the directory and calendars and an operator activates
packages, but only a staff or supervisor profile can decide.

## What does a ReviewRequest pin?

A producer names a [ReviewPolicy](../../reference/glossary/#review-policy) by `id`, and Casework
freezes the policy's concrete binding when it accepts the request:

```json test-excerpt
{
  "binding": {
    "digest": "<digest>",
    "id": "decision",
    "version": "1"
  },
  "context": {
    "mode": "submitted"
  },
  "contextCustody": "admitted bounded snapshot",
  "stages": [
    {
      "decidingProfiles": [
        "staff"
      ],
      "excludeInitiator": false,
      "excludePreviousStageReviewers": false,
      "id": "answer",
      "queue": "decisions",
      "requiredApprovals": 1
    }
  ]
}
```

The request also binds its subject by `source`, `type`, `id`, `version`, and a content `digest`.
The acceptance returns a `submissionDigest` over the producer, its source namespace, and the whole
request. A retry with the same `Idempotency-Key` recovers that acceptance, and a later policy change
never reinterprets it.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-review-protocol/src/lib.rs, ReviewCreateRequest, submission_digest,
    and ReviewRequestAccepted; crates/registry-casework/src/pinned_work.rs; products/casework/README.md. */}

## Where does the reviewer's context come from?

A policy chooses one of two custody models, and `contextCustody` names it.

With `context.mode: submitted`, the producer sends a snapshot that Casework validates against the
policy's `displaySchema` and keeps. Casework runs without any source service, and the snapshot is
review input, not current source data.

With `context.mode: current-source`, the policy names a context provider, the source's read-only
review interface, listed under `contextProviders` (empty in your project). Casework keeps an exact
reference and task metadata, never the source's data. Each time a reviewer opens a task, Casework
asks the source again under that reviewer's own credential, and `contextCustody` reads
`fresh human caller read; source values are ephemeral`. When the source conceals the subject or its
version moved, Casework refuses the read rather than show stale data.

Either way the source keeps its data and its disclosure. Casework holds no source write or apply
authority, so a review never becomes a change at the source by itself.

## Who may decide at each stage?

A policy's `stages` run in order. Each names its [queue](../../reference/glossary/#queue), the
profiles that may decide, and how many independent reviewers must approve. When a stage becomes
active, Casework creates a [ReviewTask](../../reference/glossary/#review-task). A person sees it only
as a member of a directory team serving that queue, holding a deciding profile, and not excluded as
the initiator or an earlier stage's reviewer.

Approvals that meet the threshold advance the request, or settle it at the last stage; a rejection,
a request for changes, or an answer settles it at once. Each decision is fenced by the task's
`revision`, so a decision made on a stale read is refused.

## What does the result carry?

The ReviewResult repeats the subject, the policy binding, and the `submissionDigest`, so the
producer can match it to its request, and adds a terminal `status`, the `outcome`, any public
reason, and any structured result the policy requires. It never names the reviewer or carries the
reviewer's private reason, which stays in Casework's accountability record.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-review-protocol/src/lib.rs, ReviewResult;
    crates/registry-casework-core/src/review.rs, DecisionKind; products/casework/README.md. */}

## How do clocks and calendars fit?

A clock measures a subject or a stage against a deadline, in elapsed time or in working days from
a calendar and its holiday sets. It can send a reminder or move a due task to another queue, and
never decides an outcome; [Author a Casework policy](../../configure/casework/) shows how to declare them.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-casework-core/src/clock_runtime.rs, ClockNextEffect;

## How long does Casework keep it?

`terminalRetentionDays` bounds how long the producer can read a result, and
`accountabilityRetentionDays`, never shorter, bounds the protected record of who decided. Maintenance
erases each once it expires; [Review retention and audit](../../operate/casework-retention/) covers how
you verify it.
