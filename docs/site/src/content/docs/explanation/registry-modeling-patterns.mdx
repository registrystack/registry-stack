If you are designing your own Base Registry Engine (BReg) project, start with the
questions your registry must answer and the changes callers may make.
Use Record types for stored facts, references for relationships, and Actions
when a change needs an explicit plan.
Keep domain meaning, write policy, and disclosure separate so that adopting a
record structure does not accidentally adopt another institution's authority.

## Patterns become ordinary configuration

BReg has no built-in facility, business, permit, or authority model.
You declare Record types under `recordTypes`, with fields, references, dataset
membership, and mutation rules.
The [minimal directory project](../../tutorials/first-breg/)
uses ordinary record maintenance without Actions or an external review service.
The [facility project](../../configure/breg/#start-from-a-complete-project)
adds atomic registration and durable corrections to the same record model.
Both are complete projects, with explicit grants and synthetic journeys you can adapt.

Keep an object separate from its registration when their lifecycles differ.
A facility can change operator without becoming a different facility.
A business can hold several permits, and a permit's expiry need not end the business.
Separate records let you express those changes without replacing the object's identity.

## Identifiers

Keep a scheme identifier, such as a business registration number, separate from
the server's record ID.
The issuing scheme determines what the number means and whether it can be reassigned.
Use strings for values such as `0017` so their leading zeroes survive.

A permanent identifier can be a field on its holder.
Use a separate identifier Record type when one holder can have several identifiers,
or assignments have their own issuer, validity period, or replacement history.
Choose uniqueness from the scheme's rules: two authorities may both issue `0017`,
while one shared namespace may prohibit that duplicate regardless of issuer.
BReg's composite unique constraints can express a key made from several fields.
Adding a predecessor reference does not by itself make that predecessor inactive.
Test duplicate assignments and document scheme-specific normalization;
normalization is not proof that two records describe the same real-world object.

## Relationships with dates

Use a relationship record when the link has information of its own.
The facility project separates `facility-assignment` from `facility` and
`operator`, with typed references to both.
An immediate registration Action creates the facility and its assignment together.
That starter records the initial assignment; it does not impose a dated transfer policy.

{/* Evidence: products/breg/examples/facility/registry.yaml;
    crates/registry-breg/tests/refactor_contract.rs,
    one_action_family_compiles_both_modes_and_a_neutral_dag(). */}

For dated assignments, decide separately when a link applies and which concurrent
links your policy allows.
One facility might allow one operator, several operators, or one primary operator
alongside other roles.
A `temporal` declaration identifies validity boundaries; exclusivity needs a
separate `temporal-non-overlap` constraint with the appropriate `scopeFields`.
A date or role field alone does not impose exclusivity.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-breg/src/contract.rs, TemporalSource and ConstraintSource;
    crates/registry-breg/tests/compiler_contract.rs,
    temporal_validity_compiles_without_non_overlap_constraint(). */}

Keep effective time separate from recording time.
An assignment that began on June 1 and was entered on June 10 has two relevant dates.
"Who operated the facility on June 1?" differs from "what had the registry recorded on June 1?"
Test adjacent and open-ended periods, permitted concurrency, and a correction to
an earlier period as well as a transfer beginning later.

## Choose how changes reach records

Ordinary record routes suit contact or directory maintenance when authorized
callers can create and patch each record directly.
Use an immediate Action when one authorized operation must commit several effects
atomically, such as registration and its initial assignment.
Use a Proposal Action when work needs a durable draft and later application.

The facility project keeps two choices independent:
`deactivate-facility` requires no external review and waits for a separately
authorized manual Apply; `correct-address` requires independent review and uses
automatic application.
A Proposal is useful even when external review is unnecessary.
Review approval alone never establishes that the source change was applied.

{/* Evidence: products/breg/examples/facility/registry.yaml;
    products/breg/examples/facility/tests/journeys.yaml;
    crates/registry-breg/tests/postgres_facility_proposal.rs,
    canonical_rhai_correction_freezes_reviews_applies_and_recovers_locally(). */}

Choose a fixed plan when its effects can be declared directly.
Use a named Function when computing the plan needs local logic.
The Function declares its runtime, source, entrypoint, capabilities, and limits;
the calling Action bounds its inputs, writes, and refusals.
The facility address Function computes a bounded patch or returns its declared
postal-code refusal, within the Action's write ceiling.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-breg/src/contract.rs, ActionPlanSource and FunctionSource;
    products/breg/examples/facility/plans/correct-address.rhai;
    crates/registry-breg/tests/refactor_contract.rs,
    canonical_address_function_evaluates_valid_inputs_and_declared_refusal(). */}

Use the Record type's `mutationPolicy` when an operation must require a Proposal,
independently of a caller's grants.
An immediate Action cannot bypass `proposal-required`.
Keep current target checks in `commitGuards`, such as an unchanged revision and
an active facility status.
[Declare Actions and Proposals](../../configure/breg-change-control/) explains
these choices and the separate review and application powers.

## Declarations and observations

Keep a reported claim separate from the object it describes when the claim has
its own reporter, period, measurement, or supporting material.
A facility discharge declaration can name a substance, quantity, unit, reporting
period, and reporter without replacing the facility's current state.
An inspection observation and an operator's declaration can disagree without one
automatically overwriting the other.

Decide whether correction means patching an authorized mutable record or adding
a successor that explicitly references the earlier claim.
A `create-only` Record type permits creation without later patching; your domain
rules still determine which claim counts as current and what a replacement means.
Use a Proposal when the correction needs durable submission or review.

## Source provenance

For imported contributions, retain the source system, source record ID and
version, and mapping revision.
Keep contributions separate from local authoritative records when sources can
disagree or reprocessing must remain traceable.
Decide whether an exact replay is a duplicate and whether a new mapping revision
creates a new contribution.
Record-level provenance cannot explain individual values unless you also retain
the source content or field-level contributions.

## Document references

For an inspection PDF stored elsewhere, retain the object identity and immutable
version, media type, and calculated digest, with an explicit inspection reference.
A digest supports a comparison of bytes; it does not identify the issuer or prove
the document's contents.
Keep storage, retrieval authorization, and authenticity verification with their
owning components.
Permission to read reference metadata does not imply permission to retrieve the file.
Retain original material when the inspection's history depends on it and explicitly
relate corrected replacements.

For attachments retained with a Proposal, use the bounded attachment policy in
[Actions and Proposals](../../reference/breg-api/#proposals) rather than treating
an ordinary string field as file storage.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-breg/src/contract.rs, AttachmentSlotSource;
    products/breg/examples/facility/registry.yaml. */}

## Public authorities and responsibilities

Give an authority its own identity when registrations or permits refer to it.
Separate the organisation from a dated responsibility for a jurisdiction or service.
A rename may preserve identity; a merger may create a successor.
Record those domain rules explicitly rather than inferring succession from names.
Different authorities can perform different functions in the same jurisdiction,
so jurisdiction alone is not always a suitable exclusivity scope.

## Preserve meaning when values are incomplete

Missing, unknown, not applicable, and withheld values have different meanings.
Use explicit reason codes when that distinction matters; do not turn every absence
into zero or `false`.
"No declaration received" differs from "declared quantity is zero."
Preserve approximate dates as approximate: a known year is not a known January 1.
BReg's `date` field represents a full calendar date, so less precise information
needs a deliberately different representation.
Keep registered names separate from abbreviations and translated display names
when those distinctions matter to your queries.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-breg/src/data.rs, valid_iso_date(). */}

## Adapt a pattern without inheriting policy accidentally

Keep a small model in the project file when that is sufficient.
Use modules for definitions that share meaning across projects.
Module extensions add fields and constraints; they cannot remove an inherited
constraint or replace an existing field.
If the shared policy does not fit, change the owning definition rather than
expecting an extension to relax it.

{/* Evidence: crates/registry-breg/src/contract.rs, RegistryModule and EntityExtensionSource;
    crates/registry-breg/src/compiler.rs, merge_extension();
    crates/registry-breg/tests/compiler_contract.rs,
    additive_module_conflicts_fail_instead_of_using_input_precedence(). */}

Review access separately from structure.
Record reads, Action invocation, and Proposal transitions have separate grants.
A classification label or caller-supplied query filter is not an authorization boundary.
Inspect module access contributions and related reads before adopting them.
The facility project's related operator read uses the selected facility profile;
its direct operator read has a separately authored grant.

{/* Evidence: products/breg/examples/facility/registry.yaml;
    products/breg/examples/facility/README.md;
    crates/registry-breg/src/access.rs, explain_access(). */}

Pair the model with synthetic records, allowed and refused operations, a correction
journey, and an upgrade case.
Include a case your policy permits that a stricter model might accidentally reject.
Review identity, uniqueness, required fields, and permissions separately from
optional metadata additions.
